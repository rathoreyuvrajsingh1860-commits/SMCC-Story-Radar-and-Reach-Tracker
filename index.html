SMCC Story Radar and Reach Tracker

A decision-support prototype for the Science Media Communication Cell. It suggests which lab outputs deserve a story, and it tracks what happens after a story goes out.

Overview Story Radar Reach Tracker

The problem

SMCC carries research from Indian labs to the public through TV, radio, print, digital and social media. Two parts of that work look manual: finding which outputs are worth a story, and learning what a story achieved once it is out. The CSIR News portal feed shows why: most entries I read were events, awards and MoUs, with research outputs mixed in among them.

How the tool works

  1. CollectGather recent public outputs from chosen labs: papers, patents, announcements.
  2. ScoreRate each for public benefit, novelty, visual potential, timeliness and regional relevance. Editors set the weights.
  3. ReviewAn editor reads a cited draft brief, edits it, then approves or rejects it.
  4. TrackApproved stories go into a log where coverage and enquiries are recorded.

What this prototype does

  • Ranks six real items from the CSIR News portal live as you change the weights on the Story Radar tab.
  • Drafts an editable template brief and suggests a format for each output.
  • Drafts a fuller brief with Claude on request, using only the blurb and any source text you paste. Every AI draft is marked for checking and lists the claims to verify.
  • Translates a brief into Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil or Telugu on request. The translation is marked as machine output for a fluent editor to check.
  • Moves approved stories to the Reach Tracker, where you enter mentions and enquiries and see totals.
  • Keeps your choices in this browser only.

What it does not do yet

  • It does not collect items by itself. The six items are a one-time snapshot of the portal feed, and the ratings are provisional guesses that editors can change.
  • It does not find coverage on its own. Mentions are entered manually here.
  • It publishes nothing. Every brief needs an editor’s approval and a source link.

How we would judge a full version

  • How many of SMCC’s past stories the Radar would have flagged.
  • How many drafted briefs editors keep, and how much time each saves.
  • How many placed stories have any downstream mention found.
I found no public sign of a tool like this at SMCC, but internal tools are invisible from outside. Confirm with SMCC before calling it new.
